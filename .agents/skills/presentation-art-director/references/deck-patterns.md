# Genre-specific presentation design

## Executive / consulting
For decision makers: cover → answer-first executive summary → situation/opportunity → evidence → options → recommendation → decision/next steps → appendix, adapted as required. Assertive, fact-supported titles. Compact graphs with footnotes. Design may be editorial, not necessarily corporate-blue.

## Investor pitch
Problem → why now → solution/product → market → business model → traction → differentiation → team → financing / ask. Favor narrative momentum and authentic product imagery; never invent traction or market figures.

## Research / analytical
Objectives/scope → method/data → supported findings → comparisons → charts → limitations → implications → source appendix. A read-alone deck may be substantially denser than a live talk.

## Brand / creative
Position/mood → visual idea → proof of relevance → identity → real applications → executions. Bold layout, photography, typography and rhythm; avoid arbitrary business charts.

## Educational
Learning objective → accessible explanation → labeled diagrams → example → application → takeaway. Progressively reveal complexity.

## Density: working starting points, NOT rules
Approximate area of text bounding boxes as percentage of slide, not glyph pixels:
- Brand: 5–20%, roughly 5–20 words.
- Live pitch: 10–25%, roughly 10–30 words.
- Executive: 20–40%, roughly 20–50 words.
- Read-alone analytics: 30–55%, roughly 40–90 words.
Use more or less wherever meaning, accessibility, language length, evidence, footnotes or audience require. Do not erase nuance to meet a target.

## Visual diversity without inconsistency
Choose genuinely useful slide types: thesis statement, chart-led finding, annotated image, 2-way comparison, timeline, process diagram, photo essay, editorial type-only, dense research table, market map, source summary. Don't force each type into one deck. Cohesion requires consistent typography, spacing and deliberate visual rhythm.
