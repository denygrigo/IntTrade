# Presentation standard

A deck must look like a thoughtfully art-directed product, not a uniform AI slide template. Choose purpose and audience first, decide live vs read-alone, research accessible real references, then commit to one design system for this particular deck.

- An executive deck uses decision-ready claims and clear source-backed visuals; a pitch tells a convincing story; research prioritizes evidence and caveats; brand work uses controlled emotion; educational decks explain progressively.
- Originality means differences in structure, editorial typography, composition and data grammar, not changing only colors or stock images.
- Real images: actual subject or a licensed image with adequate resolution/crop/contrast. Charts and diagrams often communicate better than decoration.
- Do not assume Pinterest is licensed for reuse. Adapt general styles; preserve author credit and rights status when known.
- Distinct fonts and carefully composed grids are valuable; use real fonts available in the runtime and alternatives if needed. Suggested open families: Inter, Manrope, IBM Plex, DM Sans, Plus Jakarta Sans, Lora, Libre Baskerville, Playfair Display.
- Full-page image screenshots are not editable PowerPoint. Never call plain ZIP-validation a visual inspection.
- Only call render QA complete after viewing real renders.
